ABOUT MANDALA

A quiet place to turn a big goal into movement.

Mandala starts before the ordinary to-do list. You name one meaningful goal, expose the drivers beneath it, turn those drivers into actions, decide what deserves attention, and reduce the whole structure to a useful next move. The map can grow, but the interface keeps asking the same question: what is worth doing now?

THE SPIRIT

Make the invisible visible. Turn the abstract into action.

The product borrows the discipline of Mandala-style goal decomposition and the Harada tradition of connecting ambition to repeated behavior, but it should never feel like methodology homework. The experience is intentionally lighter: arrive, name the intention, see the whole, choose the next move, act, and return with new information.

Calm before the action. Spark at the action. Calm after the action. The visual language uses space, balance, flow, natural color, and restrained ritual rather than decorative cultural imitation.

01 · CENTER

One goal, clearly named

The center is a real outcome: ship a product, earn the first customer, finish a book, prepare for an exam, organize a move, or learn a skill. A singular center keeps the map from becoming an everything-list.

02 · DRIVERS

Ask what must move

Around the goal sit eight drivers: the conditions, capabilities, systems, habits, or areas that materially support it. Eight is a productive constraint, not a claim that reality always arrives in neat groups of eight.

03 · ACTIONS

Convert outcomes into controllable behavior

A useful action is specific enough to start and clear enough to finish. If “launch the product” still feels too large, split it. If “test the payment flow” is obvious, stop planning and do it.

04 · DECIDE

The map is a possibility space, not a sentence

Not every generated action deserves execution. Mark work Do / Later / Hand off / Drop. Add Impact, Effort, or a blocker only when those signals genuinely improve the decision.

THE EXECUTION LOOP

Break it down → Decide → Do next → Review

01 Goal

Name the outcome and make success concrete enough to recognize.

02 Drivers

Expose the distinct forces that need to move for the goal to happen.

03 Actions

Translate each driver into behavior you can actually perform.

04 Decide

Narrow the possibility space instead of treating everything as equally urgent.

05 Review

Let reality change the plan. Keep what worked; revise what did not.

THREE VIEWS · ONE STATE

Think spatially, scan structurally, or return to the classic 9×9.

Map

A zoomable, pannable spatial view for recursive decomposition, context, focus, and direct manipulation.

Table

A faster decision surface for scanning titles, status, Do/Later/Hand off/Drop, Impact, Effort, and blockers.

Grid

The classic 9×9 Mandala: goal at the center, eight drivers around it, and eight actions around each driver. Available on 1024px+ screens.

NEXT MOVE

Reduce the tree to something executable

Next Move looks for unfinished, ready, concrete leaves that are still marked Do. Blocked work and intentionally deferred work stay visible without competing for immediate attention.

REVIEW

A plan is a hypothesis

Review asks what actually moved the goal, what turned out to be unnecessary, what is blocked, what changed, and what the next bottleneck is. The map should learn from reality instead of defending yesterday’s plan.

LOCAL FIRST

Multiple maps, no account required

Maps live in IndexedDB on the device. You can create several Mandalas, switch between them, keep bounded autosave snapshots, and restore older versions without needing a backend, login, subscription, or network connection.

MAPS ABOVE MAPS

Connect goals only when the connection is useful

Maps can remain completely independent, share a loose Group, be marked Related, form a Before/After sequence, or become Parent/Child goals. The outer structure is optional so unrelated parts of life never need a fake hierarchy.

PERSONAL SPACE

Four curated palettes, one visual language

Light, Dark, Sage, and Dawn change the mood without turning Mandala into a theme-builder. On phones, the product keeps Map and Table touch-friendly; the dense 9×9 Grid is intentionally kept off small screens.

AI, LATER AND CAREFULLY

Assist the plan; do not replace the person

The current Suggest feature is local demo logic. A future AI layer may expose missing drivers, rewrite vague outcomes, split oversized work, or critique weak plans—but proposed changes should remain visible and under the user’s control.

THE TEST

Did this make useful action happen?

Mandala is not successful because an 81-cell grid looks clever. It is successful when a person starts work they would otherwise postpone, completes something meaningful, comes back with new information, and updates the map. The product should help other work ship—not become an elaborate form of avoiding it.
