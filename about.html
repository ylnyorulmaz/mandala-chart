ABOUT MANDALA

Turn “I should do this” into “this is what I do next.”

Mandala is a visual decision-and-execution experiment built around one simple idea: planning should make the next move easier, not create more bureaucracy. Put the goal in the center, break it down, decide what is Do / Later / Hand off / Drop, execute a strong next move, and review the map when reality changes.

THE SPIRIT

The goal is not only the summit.

Mandala takes the practical structure of goal decomposition, but its emotional center is lighter: arrive, name the intention, practice the next move, notice what changed, and return. The plan should shape attention and character, not merely produce more metrics.

“Make the invisible visible. Turn the abstract into action.”

Harada-method motto

Inspired by the balance of mind, craft, and body: clear intention, practiced technique, and action in the real world. The interface should carry that calm without becoming solemn. Progress still gets a spark.

01 · THE CORE

One goal in the center

Start with a real outcome: ship a product, finish a book, learn a skill, prepare for an exam, organize a move, or complete a project. The center node is deliberately singular so the map starts with one clear intention.

02 · THE DRIVERS

Ask what must be true

Around the goal, define up to eight distinct drivers. These are not random tasks. They are the major conditions, capabilities, systems, or areas that materially move the central goal forward.

03 · THE ACTIONS

Convert drivers into things you can do

Open a driver and break it into concrete actions. Good actions are controllable, specific, observable, and small enough that you can tell whether they are done without holding another planning meeting with yourself.

04 · DIVIDE & CONQUER

Split only when splitting helps

“Launch the product” is not a useful task. “Test the payment flow” is. If an action still feels vague, intimidating, or too large to start, open it and split it again. Stop when the next move is obvious.

THE EXECUTION LOOP

The map is not the work. It exists to produce the work.

01 Goal

State one outcome clearly enough to know what success means.

02 Drivers

Identify the distinct forces that need to move for the goal to happen.

03 Actions

Translate each driver into concrete, controllable behavior.

04 Decide

Choose Do, Later, Hand off, or Drop. Add Impact/Effort or a blocker only when useful.

05 Do next

Play one strong ready action instead of staring at the whole plan.

WHY EIGHT?

A constraint, not a law

The interface borrows the eight-around-one structure associated with Mandala-style planning because it forces broad coverage without becoming an endless blank canvas. Eight is useful as a thinking constraint. It is not a claim that every real-world problem naturally contains exactly eight parts.

DECIDE

Not every generated task deserves your attention

The 8×8 map is a possibility space, not a sixty-four-item checklist. Make one simple decision: Do / Later / Hand off / Drop. Add Impact/Effort or a blocker only when it genuinely helps.

AI ASSISTANCE

AI should challenge the plan, not replace thinking

The current prototype uses local suggestions only. The intended AI layer can propose missing drivers, rewrite vague outcomes, detect overlaps, flag uncontrollable goals, and break oversized actions into smaller ones while leaving the final plan under the user’s control.

LOCAL FIRST

Your map works without an account

The first version stores its state locally in the browser and can run as a lightweight PWA. Accounts, cloud sync, collaboration, and model integrations can come later without making them prerequisites for the basic planning flow.

THE PRINCIPLES BEHIND IT

Several useful planning ideas, one interface

Mandala borrows useful ideas from Mandala planning, divide-and-conquer, Work Breakdown Structure, controllable-action thinking, Definition of Done, Impact/Effort, Eisenhower-style prioritization, the 4Ds, dependencies, and feedback loops. Those ideas stay under the hood. The interface should feel much simpler: Break it down. Decide. Do next. Review.

  • Prefer controllable actions over outcomes you cannot directly command.
  • Prefer specific definitions of done over vague intentions.
  • Break work down when size or ambiguity creates friction.
  • Do not decompose so far that the map becomes bureaucracy.
  • Use Do / Later / Hand off / Drop instead of pretending every generated task is mandatory.
  • Keep methodology jargon out of the main interaction.
  • Treat Drop as a decision, not destructive deletion of the node.
  • Revise the map when reality disproves the original plan.
